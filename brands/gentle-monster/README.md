## US footprint
Facts, with source dates in brackets.
- New York: first US flagship at 79 Grand Street, SoHo, opened 11 February 2016 [Newswire, 2016-02-16]. The brand later moved to 70 Wooster Street (6,000 sq ft; the 2026 Hypebeast article dates the opening to 2018) and reopened it fully redesigned in September 2026 [Essential Homme, undated; Hypebeast, 2026-09-15].
- Los Angeles: second US flagship, 5,000 sq ft at 816 S Broadway, downtown, opened October 2017 [Wallpaper, 2017-10-27]. Seasonal pop-ups on Melrose Avenue in West Hollywood in 2025 and 2026.
- Santa Clara, California (Westfield Valley Fair), 4,300 sq ft, third US store, March 2021 [Retail TouchPoints, 2021-03-03].
- Houston Galleria, 4,800 sq ft, May 2023 [MR Magazine, 2023-05-25].
- American Dream, East Rutherford, New Jersey, 4,200 sq ft, mid 2024; Chain Store Age counts six US stores at that point [Malls.com, 2024-07-31].
- Road to VR reports eight North American stores in July 2026, a goal of more than ten by end of 2026, and 86 stores worldwide, 36 of them in Korea [2026-07-21]. This figure comes from a spokesperson quoted in a secondary source.
- Wholesale: Nordstrom and Harvey Nichols were named at the 2016 SoHo opening, and Nordstrom ran a Gentle Monster Pop-In in early 2017 [W Magazine, 2017-01-11]. No current US wholesale list was found.

## Positioning and price tier compared with Yohji
Facts: Korean eyewear brand founded in 2011 that treats stores as art installations, built by an in-house team that includes a robotics lab [Yahoo/WWD, 2021-03-18]. Eyewear sold at $215 to $305 in the 2017 Nordstrom Pop-In [W Magazine, 2017-01-11]; current prices were not checked. A press piece describes backing from LVMH's private equity arm L Catterton Asia in 2017 and a reported $100 million Google investment for about 4% [Detail Digest, 2025-02-28; Inside Retail, 2025-06-30].
Inference: Different category (accessories, not apparel) and a different register. Gentle Monster is spectacle, pop culture and K-pop-led, while Yohji is quiet, craft-led and designer-led. It is also a funded, scaled group, not an independent house. The overlap is the avant-garde positioning and the idea of the store as a gallery.

## Three takeaways for Yohji
Each line is our inference from the sources above.
1. Treat the New York store as a changing stage. The Wooster store has carried a sequence of one-off installations and partner takeovers (Grand Blue in 2016, a farewell exhibition in 2018, Margiela in 2023 and 2024, a full redesign in 2026), so there is always something new to see and post. Yohji can borrow the cadence at a smaller scale, with a seasonal artist or craft installation, because it needs no new location.
2. Package a partner drop as one repeatable format. Margiela 2023 and Jentle Salon 2024 ran the same pop-up in many cities, with a pop-up gift (Margiela cushion) or accessory range (Jennie charms) and an online shop for the same collection. A single limited Yohji collaboration with an in-store-only object and an online release would give a measurable bridge between store and web. The sources do not show that Gentle Monster captures customer data, so this part is a design gap to fill.
3. Copy the mechanics, not the celebrity. The documented digital layer is social seeding (artists at Coachella) and playful tools (AI avatar booths), not an app, clienteling or loyalty scheme. Gentle Monster's own 2016 US leadership said celebrity ties did not necessarily move US sales (Business of Fashion, undated), yet it later built on Jennie. Yohji can build the owned-data layer that Gentle Monster has not shown in the US, and choose partners from its own craft and art universe.
