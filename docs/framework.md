# Benchmark framework

Each initiative gets one row and one primary family. A brand therefore yields several rows.

## Seven initiative families

| Family | Definition | Examples from our research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-off in-store experience | A single event inside the brand's own space: opening, preview, launch, talk | Wooster opening reception (Yohji); Dover Street Market Los Angeles opening party |
| Recurring experience | Programming with a cadence: rotating exhibitions, seasonal resets, drops, annual programs | Dover Street Market rotating designers; Issey Miyake's Mado gallery; Loewe Craft Prize |
| Omnichannel set-up | Permanent infrastructure linking store and online: beacons, app, QR, buy-online-pickup-in-store, clienteling, unified inventory | Wooster beacons and link to the online boutique; Uniqlo pickup and app; GU store, app and e-commerce launched together |
| Activation | A temporary initiative outside the store or in a partner space: pop-up, residency, guerrilla store | Visvim New York pop-up; Beams Los Angeles pop-ups; Just One Eye residency; Comme des Garcons Guerrilla stores |
| Digital influence | Reach and demand built through digital channels: social content, creators, live shopping, podcasts | Uniqlo live shopping with store staff; Margiela podcast; Wooster Instagram account |
| Collaboration | A partnership designed to move audiences between store and online, or to bring a partner's traffic | GU with UnderCover; Beams with Vans and Champion; Uniqlo with the New York Public Library |
| Community and loyalty | Mechanisms that build a lasting relationship: local creative networks, private clienteling, membership | Gentle Monster's gallery for local artists; The Row's private fittings; Uniqlo UTme! with local artists |

Boundary rules:
- Inside the store means experience, outside it means activation.
- With a cadence means recurring, a single event means one-off.

## Eight attributes (one column each)

1. Objective: test demand, awareness, brand statement, community, customer data, loyalty or sales
2. Audience: existing fans, local creatives, new luxury or streetwear buyers, tourists
3. Format and cadence: duration, frequency, scale (columns `dates`, `what_happened`)
4. Physical-digital bridge: what moves people between store and online, and whether customer data is captured
5. Partners: who is involved and what they bring
6. Investment: capital, team and duration; use proxies such as size and length of run when figures are not public
7. Results: reported outcomes and the brand's next move (a pop-up that led to a permanent store is a strong signal)
8. Transferability to Yohji: fit with the brand universe, cost against Yohji's means, New York feasibility, risk

## Rating scale

Transferability is rated 1 to 3 and Results are described in words. 3 means strong evidence or strong fit, 2 means partial, 1 means weak or not found. Write one line of reasoning in `results_and_next_move` or in your brand takeaways.

## Brand-level criteria (for choosing brands)

Six criteria screen the brands before we mine them. Ratings of 1 to 3 are in `data/brand_scores.csv`.

- Proximity: designer-led, sharp aesthetic, high prices, niche fan base
- US situation: a Japanese or avant-garde brand entering or re-entering the US with few points of sale
- Mechanics: observable community mechanics (residencies, pop-ups, collaborations, store linked to online)
- Results: visible traction, longevity or a well-documented failure
- Transferability: resources close to Yohji's, meaning an independent house rather than a group
- Sources: verifiable public pages

## What we can observe per family

Public data is thin, so use proxies:
- One-off experiences: guest quality, press coverage
- Recurring experiences: number of editions, how long the program has run
- Omnichannel set-ups: which features exist, how long they have been live
- Activations: duration and the follow-on step
- Digital influence: follower counts, creator mix
- Collaborations: sell-through, press reaction
